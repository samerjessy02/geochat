# City Data Guide & Gazetteer (Synthetic)

*This is a fictional companion document for the synthetic Cairo-inspired GIS dataset. All names, dates, figures and facts below are invented for testing the knowledge/RAG pipeline and do not describe any real place.*

## Overview

The city is a fictional metropolitan model inspired by Cairo, straddling the **Nile River**, which flows roughly north–south and divides the city into an eastern and a western bank. The dataset covers **10 neighborhoods**, **5 universities**, **35 hospitals**, **150 pharmacies**, **300 restaurants**, **120 schools**, **40 parks**, **25 metro stations**, **120 bus stops**, **8 flood zones**, **20 shopping malls**, **40 landmarks**, **25 police stations** and **15 fire stations**, connected by a network of **15 major roads**. Downtown is the dense commercial core; the University District concentrates higher education on the west bank; and the Industrial Zone sits on the city's southern edge.

The city was formally chartered in 1906 and today has an estimated population of 4.6 million. Its administrative center is Downtown, and the historic core is Old Town.

## Neighborhoods

### Downtown
Downtown is the commercial and administrative heart of the city, holding the highest concentration of restaurants, offices and metro stations. It hosts City Mall, the main civic buildings, and Downtown School. Roughly 178 of the city's restaurants cluster here, making it the densest dining district. Downtown lies just east of the Nile and has moderate flood exposure, with Flood Zones 3, 4 and 5 falling within its boundaries.

### University District
The University District, on the west bank, is the city's education hub. It contains four of the five universities — Cairo University, El Nahda University, Al Azhar Science University and Helwan Technical University — along with dense student housing, cafés and pharmacies. The district has a large daytime population that swells during term time and is well served by the metro.

### Nasr City
Nasr City is a large planned residential neighborhood on the eastern side, known for schools, parks and family housing. It has one of the highest concentrations of schools in the city and is considered a quiet commuter district.

### Nile Riverside
Nile Riverside is a commercial strip running along the east bank of the Nile. Because it borders the river directly, it carries the highest flood risk in the city: Flood Zone 1 (medium) and Flood Zone 2 (high) both lie here. It is popular for waterfront restaurants and promenades.

### Zamalek Isle
Zamalek Isle is an affluent commercial district near the river and home to New Cairo International University. It is picturesque but flood-prone, containing Flood Zones 6 and 7, both rated high risk.

### Old Town
Old Town is the historic quarter, home to many of the city's oldest landmarks, mosques and heritage buildings. It contains Flood Zone 8 (high risk) and is a major tourist draw.

### Maadi Gardens
Maadi Gardens is a leafy residential neighborhood favored by families and expatriates, characterized by tree-lined streets, parks and low-rise housing.

### Helwan Gardens
Helwan Gardens is a residential district on the western outskirts, mixing housing with green space. It is calmer and less dense than the central neighborhoods.

### New Cairo Heights
New Cairo Heights is a modern suburban district on the eastern edge, with newer housing developments, shopping malls and wide arterial roads. It is one of the fastest-growing parts of the city.

### Industrial Zone
The Industrial Zone occupies the southern edge of the city and concentrates warehouses, logistics facilities and light manufacturing. It has the highest density of fire stations, reflecting its industrial fire-safety needs, and relatively few residents.

## Universities

### Cairo University
Cairo University is the city's flagship public institution, founded in 1908 and located in the University District on the west bank. With roughly 155,000 enrolled students, it is by far the largest university in the dataset. Its best-known faculties are medicine, engineering, law and science, and it anchors the surrounding student neighborhood of cafés, pharmacies and housing.

### Al Azhar Science University
Al Azhar Science University, established in 1961, sits in the University District and enrolls about 42,000 students. It is known for the natural sciences and theological studies and is the second-oldest university in the city.

### Helwan Technical University
Helwan Technical University, founded in 1975 in the University District, focuses on engineering and applied technology. It has approximately 28,000 students and maintains close ties with the Industrial Zone for internships and applied research.

### El Nahda University
El Nahda University is a private institution founded in 1998 in the University District, enrolling around 18,000 students. It specializes in business, media and the arts and is popular for its evening programs.

### New Cairo International University
New Cairo International University is the newest and smallest of the five, a private university founded in 2015 in Zamalek Isle. It has about 9,500 students and offers internationally accredited programs in partnership with overseas institutions.

## Healthcare

The city operates **35 hospitals**, most open 24 hours a day, distributed across all neighborhoods with a cluster in and around Downtown. Together they provide an estimated 8,400 hospital beds.

### Om Kolthoum General Hospital
Om Kolthoum General Hospital is the largest hospital in the city, a 720-bed general hospital in the Downtown district that serves as the main trauma and emergency center. It runs a 24/7 emergency department and is the primary referral hospital for the central neighborhoods.

### Al Razi General Hospital
Al Razi General Hospital is a teaching hospital near the University District, closely affiliated with Cairo University's faculty of medicine. It is a center for surgical training and has about 480 beds.

### Ibn Sina General Hospital
Ibn Sina General Hospital specializes in cardiac and vascular care and is regarded as the city's leading heart center, with a dedicated catheterization unit and roughly 350 beds.

## Transport

### Metro
The city's metro system runs a single primary line of **25 stations** that cuts through Downtown, linking the western University District to the eastern suburbs. Trains run from roughly 05:00 to 01:00 daily, and the busiest interchange is in Downtown.

### Roads and buses
A network of 15 major roads, including a peripheral Ring Road, forms the connected backbone of the city. Several major roads cross the Nile by bridge. **120 bus stops** are positioned along these major roads, providing surface transit that complements the metro.

### The Nile River
The Nile River flows north–south through the middle of the city, separating the western bank (home to the University District and Helwan Gardens) from the eastern bank (Nasr City, New Cairo Heights). Seven major roads cross it by bridge. Riverside districts benefit from waterfront amenities but bear the city's highest flood risk.

---

## Flood Zones

The city delineates **8 flood zones**, all along the Nile corridor, each rated low, medium or high risk. Three zones are rated **high risk**: Flood Zone 2 (Nile Riverside), Flood Zone 6 and Flood Zone 7 (Zamalek Isle), and Flood Zone 8 (Old Town). Flood Zones 1, 4 and 5 are rated **medium**, and Flood Zone 3 (Downtown) is the only **low-risk** zone. Riverside and island districts — Nile Riverside, Zamalek Isle and parts of Old Town — carry the greatest exposure, and several city parks fall partly inside these zones.

## Landmarks & institutions

### El Gomhoria Opera House
El Gomhoria Opera House is the city's principal cultural venue, located in Old Town, hosting orchestral, opera and ballet performances.

### El Tahrir Mosque
El Tahrir Mosque is one of the oldest and most visited mosques in Old Town, noted for its historic minaret.

### City Mall
City Mall is the largest shopping center in the city, opened in 2010 in the Downtown district. It houses about 220 shops across five floors, along with a cinema and food court, and is one of the busiest retail destinations.

### Downtown School
Downtown School is the oldest school in the city, founded in 1952 in the Downtown district. It is a well-known language school and a landmark of the central education system.
